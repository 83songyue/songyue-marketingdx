# Anti-Patterns

Use these public anti-patterns to name what is weak in a proposal. The diagnosis should not merely label the problem; it should explain the visible evidence and propose a sharper move.

## 1. Social Background Pretending To Be Insight

Weak signal: The plan says people are stressed, young people want self-expression, consumers pursue quality life, or everyone wants to be understood.

Why it is weak: It describes a broad atmosphere, not a decision tension.

Sharper move: Name a specific person in a specific situation facing a specific conflict.

## 2. Audience Tag Pretending To Be Audience Understanding

Weak signal: The target is defined by age, city tier, income, gender, or interests only.

Why it is weak: Labels do not explain why anyone would act.

Sharper move: Replace demographic description with scenario and trigger: "When X happens, this person struggles with Y, so they need Z."

## 3. Beautiful Words Pretending To Be Proposition

Weak signal: The proposition uses words like warmth, better life, passion, companionship, premium, lifestyle, or youth without a hard choice.

Why it is weak: Any competitor can say the same thing.

Sharper move: Turn the positive adjective into a point of view about what the brand believes or changes.

## 4. Theme Pretending To Be Creative Idea

Weak signal: The plan has a campaign theme but no phrase, symbol, action, event, scene, or contrast that people can remember.

Why it is weak: A theme can organize a deck without creating memory.

Sharper move: Create one retellable handle.

## 5. Brand Logo Pretending To Be Brand Role

Weak signal: The brand appears in the last shot, as sponsor, as blessing, or as product packshot only.

Why it is weak: Removing the brand does not damage the idea.

Sharper move: Make the brand, product, service, or channel part of the reason the action can happen.

## 6. Channel List Pretending To Be Communication Design

Weak signal: The plan lists short video, social seeding, influencers, offline pop-up, livestream, coupons, and private traffic without one main action.

Why it is weak: Channels distribute an idea; they are not the idea.

Sharper move: Define one main action and assign each channel a role around it.

## 7. Hot IP Pretending To Be Sales Logic

Weak signal: The plan assumes a famous IP, meme, celebrity, or cultural symbol will naturally create conversion.

Why it is weak: Attention does not automatically explain why this product, this channel, or this purchase moment matters.

Sharper move: Separate "does the IP/content attract attention?" from "does it help this product sell here and now?"

## 8. Public Value Pretending To Be PR Mechanism

Weak signal: The brand declares a social value, public attitude, or industry mission, but users and media have no concrete action or proof to repeat.

Why it is weak: Public words without a public mechanism remain a manifesto.

Sharper move: Build a proof point, user action, reportable event, or participation mechanism.

## 9. Promotion Pretending To Be Growth Strategy

Weak signal: The plan says coupon, discount, QR code, landing page, or membership points, but does not explain why users should move to the next step.

Why it is weak: Conversion tools are not motivation.

Sharper move: Connect audience tension, offer, channel, and next action.

## 10. Deck Completeness Pretending To Be Logic

Weak signal: The proposal has market analysis, audience, insight, proposition, creative, media, timeline, and budget, but each part could be swapped without consequence.

Why it is weak: A complete deck is not a necessary chain.

Sharper move: Rebuild the shortest path from problem to action: because A, therefore B, therefore C.

## 11. Emotional Story Pretending To Be Brand Relevance

Weak signal: The story is moving, funny, or culturally timely, but the brand does not change the story.

Why it is weak: Good content can still be bad brand communication.

Sharper move: Ask what only the brand/product/service contributes to the emotional turn.

## 12. Metric Decoration Pretending To Be Effectiveness

Weak signal: The plan fills slides with impressions, interactions, CPM, CPA, search lift, or conversion estimates without naming the core behavior change.

Why it is weak: Metrics measure something after the fact; they do not by themselves prove strategic effectiveness.

Sharper move: Define one main goal and the observable behavior that would show progress.
